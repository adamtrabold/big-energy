### 2. Flows the form must serve
- **F-pack:** switch off → keyboard off (or stays on, if the carry holds it) → panel protected → into the backpack, any orientation.
- **F-set:** out of the bag → into the playing pose in one move, no adjustment to remember.
- **F-play-table:** module angled behind or beside the keyboard; cable short, not crossing the panel or speaker.
- **F-play-lap / grass:** the module on a knee, a bench slat or grass; still angled, still stable when a knob is turned (a fingertip push of a few newtons must not tip it).
- **F-plug:** host port faces the keyboard in every pose; headphones and power don't end up under the module.
- **F-go-light:** module alone (keyboard at home, or play from a computer); the form still makes sense.

### 3. Structural questions
- **Q1 Which plane?** CATHEDRAL had two planes because the keys took the base. Now the single plane is the old lid face: the upright panel with the speaker in it. That's the most meaningful cue: **floss is CATHEDRAL's lid, standing on its own.**
- **Q2 Angle by shape, honestly.** A rigid body on a flat surface rests on its convex hull, so "shape alone" gives poses, not adjustments. Three cases [calc]:
  - **One fixed angle (a wedge):** robust, no parts. It can't also lie at 0° to play.
  - **Flat *and* angled by shape (a two-facet "V" bottom, 14° between facets):** possible only if the centre of mass sits within ≈ 4–5 mm behind the ridge (COM 15–20 mm above it). That's a knife edge: a knob press tips it. **I'd rule it out** unless the director sees a way round it.
  - **Two poses ≥ ≈ 60° apart** (e.g. on its base at 14°, or stood on its back edge nearly upright): both robust. So "shape alone" naturally gives *angled + upright*, not *flat + angled*.
  - So: if he wants flat **and** angled, something moves (cover, bail, stand). If angled is enough, shape alone does it. That's the trade-off for him (§3 Q2 → open question 1).
- **Q3 How does the keyboard come along without being part of the module?** Four kinds, independent of the form: **k1** a strap or band (is a strap "stuff" like knobs? open question); **k2** back-to-back on the family S4 inserts with printed end clips; **k3** riding on the outside of a cover; **k4** a separate printed carrier that also does something else (see SLED). Job 3's rule (held by the ends, nothing across the keys) stands.
- **Q4 Does it need a cover at all?** CATHEDRAL's rim stood 1 mm over the knob tops [eng]. A rim like that is already a guard. A cover earns its place only if it does a second job (stand, keyboard carrier, speaker baffle).
- **Q5 Which CATHEDRAL cues carry structure, not just looks?**
  - **The framed plate:** a printed rim around an inset cream plate. Structural: it guards the knobs, stiffens the shell, and hides the plate's seam.
  - **The raised panel:** the lid stood at an angle to face the player. Keep the angle; lose the hinge.
  - **The stay:** CATHEDRAL's brass leg. Now it can be **printed metal** (UNVERIFIED: which service, bronze or steel, cost). A candidate for whatever sets the angle.
  - **Soft architectural corners** ("like how Apple does theirs"): continuous-curvature corners print well and are kind to hands and bags.
  - **The pipe-line grille:** vertical slots over the speaker. Structural if the slots are the bars of the speaker box's front.
  - **Bays [repeat]:** a nave is the same bay repeated. See NAVE below.
  - **Not carried:** the bookcloth spine (he called it weird); the two-part fold (there are no keys to fold onto).
- **Q6 Size.** The turned-down 280 × 105 × 35 hull is history. Fixed points: knob stack ≈ 35 tall at the front [eng], speaker E ≈ 190 cm³ net with a ≈ 140 mm port [eng], ports on the edges. A 14° wedge over 105 deep rises ≈ 25 mm, so 35 at the front, ≈ 61 at the back, and that adds ≈ 385 cm³ over a flat 35 mm body [calc]. **That's twice the speaker box E needs.** So in a wedge, the angle *is* the speaker box, and the plan could shrink (shallower, or shorter). Worth eng's look.

### 4. Directions (differ in kind; each names its keyboard answer)
None is a favourite. The owner's two ideas are in here as **OWNER IDEA**, beside others.

**1. WEDGE: the angle is the body.** PROPOSAL, includes OWNER IDEA (angle by shape, no moving parts).
- A fixed section, thin at the front, tall at the back [14°]. The tall back is the speaker box and port (§3 Q6). No hinge, no leg.
- **Second pose:** stood on its back face, the panel goes nearly upright (≈ 76°), a "retable" for a shelf, a friend across the table, or the knee.
- I/O moves to the two **ends**, because the back face is a foot in pose 2. The host port is at the front-left end, toward the keyboard.
- **Cover:** none. The rim guards the knobs.
- **Keyboard:** k2 (back-to-back under the base) or k1.
- **Costs:** no 0° pose; thicker at the back in the bag; on soft grass it rests on its whole base, which is good.
- "Mechanicalize something idiosyncratic": his "idk just by shape" becomes the one fixed number in the case: the section.

**2. TRIPTYCH: the cover is the stand.** PROPOSAL, includes OWNER IDEA (folding cover). [triptych]
- A reliquary closes its wings over the relic and opens them to stand it up.
- One printed cover hinged along the module's back edge. **Closed:** it guards the panel in the bag. **Folded under, flat:** 0°. **Folded under in a triangle**, held by a printed ridge or magnets (magnets are "stuff"; the ridge is printed): the angle.
- Gives **flat and angled**, with one hinge (print-in-place or a printed pin).
- **Keyboard:** k3, on the cover's outside, held at the ends; it comes off to play.
- **Costs:** a moving part; a hinge to make; the cover's open position must not block the rear I/O or the speaker.

**3. BAIL: the handle is the stay.** PROPOSAL. [skillet]
- A skillet is one piece with a handle; old radios and cooking pots have a swinging bail.
- A U-shaped **printed-metal bail**, pivoting at the module's two ends. **Up:** a carry handle (park walks). **Swung down behind:** a stay at a printed detent [14°]. **Folded flat** against the back: 0°.
- **The CATHEDRAL stay becomes the bail**, now with two jobs instead of one.
- **Keyboard:** the bail is also the anchor. A keyboard clipped under the base rides along (k2); or a strap loops through the bail (k1).
- **Costs:** one moving part with detents (printed-metal detent wear: UNVERIFIED); on grass the bail's two ends dig in, which is fine or bad depending on the ground.

**4. SLED: the angle lives in the keyboard's carrier.** PROPOSAL. Invert: the module stays dumb and flat. [loom] ["remove specifics"]
- A loom holds its cloth between two beams; the cloth isn't part of either.
- The module is a plain flat slab (one plane, 0°). The **keyboard carrier** is a printed sled: it holds the keyboard by its ends and has a raised seat behind it. Drop the module onto the seat and it sits at the angle behind the keys. Without the sled, the module sits flat.
- **Keyboard:** k4. Never attached to the module; the sled is optional.
- Angle only with the sled; flat without it. Packs as two flat layers.
- **Costs:** the angle depends on bringing the sled; two objects; the sled is a family part, so it must take every module size (fine: it's the S1 keyboard envelope plus a seat).

**5. RETABLE: stand it up.** PROPOSAL. Invert: steeper, not shallower.
- An altarpiece stands on its predella. CATHEDRAL's raised lid was nearly this.
- The module stands on a thick foot, panel at ≈ 60–75°, like a tabletop radio. Lying on its back: 0° (and it packs that way).
- Speaker fires straight at the ear: the best placement for sound of the five.
- **Keyboard:** in front, on its own; carried k2 against the module's back.
- **Costs:** knobs turned from the front, like a radio, not from above; less natural on a lap; the foot holds the I/O.

**Cross-cutting: NAVE, a case of identical bays.** PROPOSAL. [repeat]
- The case is two end caps plus *n* identical printed **bay** shells, joined along the axis. Each bay is one section of the panel (synth bays → effects bay → output bay), and the joints are the section dividers.
- Why it's structural:
  - every part fits a 256 mm printer bed (eng: a one-piece 280 doesn't);
  - the seams hide in the dividers (eng already suggested splitting there);
  - **the family scales by bay count:** floss *n* bays, Big Energy more;
  - one repair part.
- It works with any of the five sections above.
- **Costs:** seams in a speaker box (keep the box its own airtight part, as eng said); an identical shell for sections with different control counts (same shell, different plate). UNVERIFIED: joint stiffness.

### 6. Open questions
1. **For the owner (the real fork):** must floss also sit **flat** to play, or is *angled* enough? Angled-only lets shape alone do it (WEDGE, RETABLE). Flat *and* angled needs one moving part (TRIPTYCH, BAIL) or a second object (SLED).
2. Is a fabric strap "stuff" like knobs (allowed), or part of the case (must be printed)?
3. Eng: printed-metal services and cost for a bail or stay (bronze vs steel); the wedge's extra volume as speaker box (does it let the plan shrink?); detent wear in printed metal.
4. Director: is the two-facet flat-and-angled shape worth chasing, given the 4–5 mm window?
5. Visual: which CATHEDRAL cue carries the look once the hinge and spine go? (I'd bet on the framed plate and the pipe-line grille.)
