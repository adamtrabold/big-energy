### 2. Flows the form must serve
- **F-pack:** switch off → keyboard off (or stays on, if the carry holds it) → panel protected → into the backpack, any orientation.
- **F-set:** out of the bag → into the playing pose in one move, no adjustment to remember.
- **F-play-table:** module angled behind or beside the keyboard; cable short, not crossing the panel or speaker.
- **F-play-lap / grass:** the module on a knee, a bench slat or grass; still angled, still stable when a knob is turned (a fingertip push of a few newtons must not tip it).
- **F-plug:** host port faces the keyboard in every pose; headphones and power don't end up under the module.
- **F-go-light:** module alone (keyboard at home, or play from a computer); the form still makes sense.

### 3. Structural questions
- **Q1 Which plane?** CATHEDRAL had two planes because the keys took the base. Now the single plane is the old lid face: the upright panel with the speaker in it. That's the most meaningful cue: **floss is CATHEDRAL's lid, standing on its own.**
- **Q2 Angle by shape, honestly.** A rigid body on a flat surface rests on its convex hull, so "shape alone" gives poses, not adjustments. Three cases [calc]:
  - **One fixed angle (a wedge):** robust, no parts. It can't also lie at 0° to play.
  - **Flat *and* angled by shape (a two-facet "V" bottom, 14° between facets):** possible only if the centre of mass sits within ≈ 4–5 mm behind the ridge (COM 15–20 mm above it). That's a knife edge: a knob press tips it. **I'd rule it out** unless the director sees a way round it.
  - **Two poses ≥ ≈ 60° apart** (e.g. on its base at 14°, or stood on its back edge nearly upright): both robust. So "shape alone" naturally gives *angled + upright*, not *flat + angled*.
  - So: if he wants flat **and** angled, something moves (cover, bail, stand). If angled is enough, shape alone does it. That's the trade-off for him (§3 Q2 → open question 1).
- **Q3 How does the keyboard come along without being part of the module?** Four kinds, independent of the form: **k1** a strap or band (is a strap "stuff" like knobs? open question); **k2** back-to-back on the family S4 inserts with printed end clips; **k3** riding on the outside of a cover; **k4** a separate printed carrier that also does something else (see SLED). Job 3's rule (held by the ends, nothing across the keys) stands.
- **Q4 Does it need a cover at all?** CATHEDRAL's rim stood 1 mm over the knob tops [eng]. A rim like that is already a guard. A cover earns its place only if it does a second job (stand, keyboard carrier, speaker baffle).
- **Q5 Which CATHEDRAL cues carry structure, not just looks?**
  - **The framed plate:** a printed rim around an inset cream plate. Structural: it guards the knobs, stiffens the shell, and hides the plate's seam.
  - **The raised panel:** the lid stood at an angle to face the player. Keep the angle; lose the hinge.
  - **The stay:** CATHEDRAL's brass leg. Now it can be **printed metal** (UNVERIFIED: which service, bronze or steel, cost). A candidate for whatever sets the angle.
  - **Soft architectural corners** ("like how Apple does theirs"): continuous-curvature corners print well and are kind to hands and bags.
  - **The pipe-line grille:** vertical slots over the speaker. Structural if the slots are the bars of the speaker box's front.
  - **Bays [repeat]:** a nave is the same bay repeated. See NAVE below.
  - **Not carried:** the bookcloth spine (he called it weird); the two-part fold (there are no keys to fold onto).
- **Q6 Size.** The turned-down 280 × 105 × 35 hull is history. Fixed points: knob stack ≈ 35 tall at the front [eng], speaker E ≈ 190 cm³ net with a ≈ 140 mm port [eng], ports on the edges. A 14° wedge over 105 deep rises ≈ 25 mm, so 35 at the front, ≈ 61 at the back, and that adds ≈ 385 cm³ over a flat 35 mm body [calc]. **That's twice the speaker box E needs.** So in a wedge, the angle *is* the speaker box, and the plan could shrink (shallower, or shorter). Worth eng's look.

**Cross-cutting: NAVE, a case of identical bays.** PROPOSAL. [repeat]

- It works with any of the five sections above.

### 4. DROP: the angle is a pipe. PROPOSAL; includes OWNER IDEA (angle by shape, no moving parts). [drop] [25°] [water] [runner]
Sketch 2 and 3 in `cathedral-jam2-sections.png`.

**Section:**
- A flat base, and a thin front lip (≈ 24 [calc]).
- The plate rising at 25° [25°].
- The back rounds into a **pipe**, Ø ≈ 72 [calc, drawn, not derived], tangent to the plate.
- From the end it's a teardrop, a drop of water lying on its side [water].

**Why the pipe, from the idea of the thing (not costume):**
- **It is the organ pipe.** Speaker E needs a ported box with a ≈ 140 mm port [eng]. The pipe is that box, and the port runs along it.
- **The port mouth sits at the centre of the end face, and the growth-ring grooves are centred on it.** The end of floss reads as the end of a log and the mouth of a pipe at once. That's CATHEDRAL's end grain with a reason.
- **Clerestory:** in a nave, the upper windows sit above the arcade. The two drivers sit in the pipe and fire forward through **lancet slots across the pipe's upper front**, above the plate's back edge. So **the plate carries controls only** (one clean plane), and **the sound comes from over the controls, toward the face**. It's one band of lancets, not two round holes.
- **The pipe is the handle.** Your hand wraps round it like a rolled newspaper. That makes BAIL unnecessary.
- **Brass runners** [runner]: two printed-metal strips along the base. These are the feet and wear strips, like the steel on a sledge's runners. A third strip sits on the rear land. Brass touches the ground and nowhere else.

**Poses:**
- **Pose 1 (play):** on its base, panel at 25°.
- **Pose 2 (shelf / friend):** on a flat **rear land** ground on the pipe, panel at ≈ 65°, facing the other way. Honest costs: in pose 2 the clerestory fires toward the table, and the centre of mass has to sit within the land's ≈ 40 mm (UNVERIFIED). Pose 2 might be one pose too many. The director can cut it.

**Keyboard and cover:**
- **Keyboard:** k2. A slim keyboard sits flat under the base, held at its ends (job 3). Packed, the kit is a drop on a plank.
- **Cover:** none. The thick rim guards the knobs, and in the bag the pipe takes the knocks.

**Look and feel:**
- Holding it should feel like picking up a well-made tool by its handle: weight low and at the back, a soft round grip, a thin front edge that slides under your fingers.
- Turning a knob should feel like a lectern: the hand rests on the low lip and the plate rises away from it.

**Volume [calc]:** a Ø 72 pipe × ≈ 270 long ≈ 1,100 cm³ gross, against E's ≈ 190 cm³ net. So either the pipe shrinks (lower angle, smaller Ø) or the cells and board live in it too. Eng's call.

**UNVERIFIED (eng):**
- two 50 mm drivers inside a Ø 72 pipe with a printable wall;
- the knob stack under a 25° plate (the board tilts with the plate, or steps).

**Rocker, considered and killed [water] [calc]:** a fully curved, self-righting belly (a keel) would bed into grass and come back to its angle by itself. **But a knob press rocks it.**
- Restoring stiffness ≈ m·g·(ρ − h) ≈ 0.6 kg × 9.8 × 0.05 m ≈ 0.3 N·m/rad (ESTIMATE: mass, radius and centre-of-mass height are guesses).
- A 3 N press 50 mm from the contact point gives 0.15 N·m ≈ **0.5 rad ≈ 29° of rock**. That's unplayable.
- So the keel needs a flat land ≳ 40 mm wide, at which point it's DROP's base. **What survives:** keep DROP's belly gently curved beyond the land, so it seats on grass and a lap. Keep it flat where it meets a table.

### 5. ROSE: differs in kind from all five. PROPOSAL. ["abandon normal instruments"] [font]

- **The angle by shape** [font]: a font is a basin tilted on its foot. Here a drum or bowl stands on a smaller **brass foot ring** (printed metal, the wear surface), cut at an angle. There are no parts, and on a lap it sits like a frame drum on the knee.
- **Honest costs:**

- **Why show it anyway:** it's the only direction where the *plan*, and not just the section, comes from CATHEDRAL. It's also the one most likely to make him want to play it (job 24) or to tell us clearly what he doesn't want.
