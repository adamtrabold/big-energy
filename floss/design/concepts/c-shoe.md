# SHOE: the shared keyboard carry (family part S9). How it works

Systems, concept building, 2026-11-22. PROPOSAL, accepted by the director (turn 5) **pending the owner**. It's an agent proposal for a family interface, not a principle. [calc] = my arithmetic; UNVERIFIED = needs checking.

## The idea
His words: "the case does not need to surround it just have an attachment point." SHOE is that attachment point, made smaller than both objects. A thin printed plate lives under the keyboard and slides into a dock under the module. The two travel back to back, keys out. Nothing crosses the keys, no part of floss reaches past the module, and any keyboard fits. The keyboard overhangs the module's ends by 23–64 mm [calc: 325–388 vs 260–280]. That overhang is the keyboard itself, not a floss part.

## Structure
- **The plate:** printed, ≈ 200 (along the keyboard) × 60 × 3. Two dovetail rails run along its 60 mm sides, and there's a printed detent bump at its back edge.
- **Keyboard ↔ plate:** removable hook-and-loop or Dual Lock pads (bought "stuff", like knobs), placed only where the keyboard's underside is flat and free. Feet, battery doors and labels differ by model: UNVERIFIED per keyboard. The card in the kit says where the pads go for the common slim models.
- **Plate ↔ module: the S9 dock.** A 200 × 60 recess with a dovetail, entered from the **front edge** and sliding **front to back**. The detent clicks at the end of its 60 mm travel, so it holds positively, never by friction alone (director condition 2). One plate and one dock on every module.
- **The direction is the same on every module (front to back).** That's what lets GNOMON take it between its cheeks, and it keeps the dock clear of CASKET-B's catch groove.

## Where the dock sits on each module
| Module | Dock | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| DROP | in the flat base, y ≈ 10–70 from the front edge, centred | base 88 deep; TPU grip strips at the front and rear edges stay clear |
| GNOMON | in a **stretcher**: a printed bar between the cheeks at table level, y ≈ 20–80 | the runners are 280 apart and the plate is 200, so it needs a bridge. The stretcher also stiffens the cheeks and floors the cable bay. **It exists only if SHOE is chosen** (director check, see `c-gnomon`) |
| CASKET-B | in the base, y ≈ 10–70, centred | the lid's catch groove is at y ≈ 80–92 across the base: **10 mm clear of the dock**. Both are recesses, so the base stays flat for the flat pose |

## Moves
- **Pack:** set the keyboard on the module's base, keys out, and slide it forward-to-back until it clicks.
- **Unpack:** pull it forward. One move.
- **Play:** the plate stays on the keyboard. **Rocking check (director condition 4):** the plate plus pads (≈ 3 + 2–3 mm [est]) must not stand taller than the keyboard's own feet, or the keyboard rocks ≈ 1–2 mm under an end key. Foot heights are UNVERIFIED per model. If a keyboard's feet are lower, peel the plate off to play (the pads allow it) or carry that keyboard loose.

## States
- **No keyboard:** the empty dock is a shallow recess. Every module still sits and packs the same.
- **A keyboard with no free 200 × 60:** use the fallbacks.
- **The plate is lost:** it's one printed part, so reprint it.

## Fallback chain (always drawn)
1. **Loose in the bag.** Every concept works this way; he said attachment "would just be nice".
2. **A printed TPU band** around the module and the keyboard, keys out, crossing only the module and the keyboard's underside side. It's printed, so it needs no "is a strap stuff?" exception.

## Owner answers it waits on
- Is a hold by the **underside** OK, instead of "by its ends" (job 3)?
- Is it OK to **stick removable pads** under his keyboard?
- If either answer is no, the fallbacks apply, and no concept changes.

## UNVERIFIED (eng)
- pad hold in shear and peel for a ≈ 0.3 kg keyboard bouncing in a bag;
- dovetail and detent tolerances in printed ASA/PETG (needs a fitting trial, so it's a named trial step);
- keyboard foot heights and free underside areas per model.
