## The idea
CATHEDRAL's lid standing on its own ends. A framed slab of constant thickness, the slab eng already proved holds everything. At each end the rim carries on down into a **cheek**. The cheek's rear face is square to the plate, so the end face has **two feet**:
- the runners give the angled pose;
- the rear face gives the upright pose.

That's his first two choices, both by shape, with no parts.

## Structure, from the end face
- **Slab:** T = 35, D = 105–115 [eng]. It holds the plate, the board, the cells at the ends, and speaker E's box in the rear region, as r3.
- **Cheek outline:** front face T (square to the plate), plate D, **rear face 2T = 70 (square to the plate)**, and the runner line closing it.
- **Angle rule, restated for the square rear face: tan θ = T / D** (the rear face is exactly 2T).
  - **θ ≈ 18.4° (D 105) / 16.9° (D 115)**, unrounded.
  - The old sin θ = T / D rule would need a 72 mm rear face (19.5° / 17.7°). I recommend tan: it keeps "2T" on a face you can see and stand on. **Director's call.**
- **Angled pose:**
  - footprint ≈ 280 × **111 (D 105) / 120 (D 115)**;
  - the back stands **≈ 66–67** tall, the front ≈ 33;
  - the footprint grows **≈ +11–12 mm** over rev 1 (99 / 110), not the ≈ 23 first estimated.
- **Upright pose:** it stands on the 280 × 70 rear face, with the plate vertical. The top edge (the front face) is **≈ 105–115** up.
- **Underside:** open between the cheeks. No façade.

## Stability [calc; mass 0.8 kg, COM ≈ 15–20 mm from the plate inside the slab, ≈ 50 up when upright: ESTIMATES]
**Angled:** as rev 1. The hand on the front edge and knob presses all land inside the footprint. Lap: runners one per thigh, the best lap answer. Grass ≈ 3.8 kPa on the runners, so the angle holds.

**Upright on a 70-deep rear face.** The plate sits at the footprint's front edge, so the COM is only ≈ 15–20 mm inside it toward the player and ≈ 50–55 toward the back.

| Load | Tips at | Read |
|---|---|---|
| Pull toward the player on the top edge | **≈ 1.0–1.5 N** | **the weak case:** a snagged cable, a careless grab. It falls forward onto its rim, which stands 1 mm over the knobs, from ≈ 110 |
| Press into a top-row knob or button (≈ 90 up) | ≈ 4–4.6 N | a firm press on HOLD or TAP may need the other hand on the top edge (tactile-switch forces are UNVERIFIED per part) |
| Press into a lower-row control (≈ 75 up) | ≈ 5.5 N | fine for turning a knob |
| Ground tilt before it falls forward | ≈ 17–22° | uneven grass is fine for standing |
| Sliding | TPU strips on the rear face | |

- **Grass upright:** good for listening and display. For playing, the loaded edge sinks, so the angled pose is better there.
- **Lap upright:** n/a.
- **What it means:** upright works by shape for **standing, listening, a shelf, a friend across the table, and light knob turns**. It isn't a hard-playing pose. It's honest for his "90" as second choice.
- **The forward-pull limit is built into the geometry:** the plate *is* the footprint's front edge, so a wider rear face doesn't help it. Only moving mass away from the plate does: cells and E's box low in the slab, toward the floor (eng; ≈ +0.07 N per mm the COM moves [calc]).
- A wider rear face helps presses only: ≈ +0.9 N at the top row per +10 mm [calc].
- So **if "90" means playing hard upright, the answer is a steadying hand, not more shape.**

## Poses (his order: angled, 90, flat)
1. **Angled (θ ≈ 17–18°) on the runners:** the main pose.
2. **Upright (90°) on the rear face:** by shape, with the limits above.
3. **Flat:** not offered (he ranked it last). Laying it on its back would stand it on the cheeks' runners and front face at a slant, so it isn't a stable flat pose. Say so; don't fake it.

## Moves: bag to sound (3)
1. Out of the bag.
2. Slide the keyboard off (or take it from the bag).
3. Set it on the runners or stand it on its rear face, plug in, switch on.

- **Undock the keyboard before standing it upright:** a deep keyboard on the stretcher could reach past the rear corner and prop it (UNVERIFIED per keyboard).
- **Packing:** a wedge 280 × 111–120 × 66–67 at the back; ≈ 81 with the keyboard docked. The docked keyboard closes the cable bay.
