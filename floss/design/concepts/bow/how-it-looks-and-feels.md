## The idea
**Life lives in each finger.** It's CATHEDRAL's walnut base and hinge, but the lid carries no controls: it's the voice. It's a walnut frame with oatmeal speaker cloth stretched over the sealed box, facing you when it stands. Below it, dark keys you lean into, and one coral knob that says what a lean does.

**Draws on:** SAIL (jam turn 2: lid as soundbox; a kite's frame and skin), the half-map (turns 2 and 6), "the keys are a fingerboard" (turn 6), CATHEDRAL (case, carrier, stays, corners, marking system), and HUSH (felt collars).

## Principles
1. **The lid is the voice, not a second panel.** *Because* floss needs about half Big Energy's controls, so the lid is free to hold the passive radiator's sealed box, and standing it fires at you. This is BOW's answer to "just a smaller CATHEDRAL?": the lid has a different job.
2. **Above each half, its own.** *Because* the split is the instrument. Octaves sit at the outer ends; each half's strands column stands beside the gutter; HOLD (left half only) sits at the left region's inner end.
3. **The keys are a fingerboard.** *Because* every note is bowed: ebony-dark naturals and bone sharps (a violin's fingerboard and nut), on felt collars so a lean gives a little.
4. **Coral is pressure.** *Because* BOW's whole idea is what your finger does after the strike.
5. **Open is on.** *Because* the lid already says "playing" or "packed". Closed covers the keys, so it can't switch on in a bag.

## Look
- **Case:** CATHEDRAL's walnut base, 286 × 132 × 27.6, with superellipse corners (case 28.3, plate 24). The cream plate is inset 4.3. Printed walnut-tone PLA first, his planed walnut later (as CATHEDRAL).
- **Lid:** CATHEDRAL's outline, ≈ 22 deep (eng), walnut.
  - **Inner face:** oatmeal speaker cloth (#e2d9c6 drawn) stretched in a 6.5 mm walnut frame. One coral thread is woven across it 20 mm from the hinge edge. **No motif and no grille shape**, because the cloth needs no hole.
  - **Outer (back) face:** CATHEDRAL's two brass impost lines, plus a **blank field (≈ 206 × 58) for the owner's own façade lines**, as he offered to draw for CATHEDRAL.
- **Keys:** CATHEDRAL's carrier and Ø 12.7 heads.
  - **Naturals:** dark-stained birch (ebony tone, #2d2522).
  - **Sharps:** bone (#efe6d2): bone-tone printed caps, or pale birch sealed matte. Eng / owner to pick.
  - Each sits on a **warm-grey felt collar** (#a39680, punched, Ø 16.7).
  - Rows: whites at y 22, sharps at y 44 (moved back 5 from my first draft so the end pegs clear the plate's 24 mm corners). **Cost for systems:** the sharps' collars now end at y 52.4, which leaves 2.6 mm to the low strand tabs (y 55), against systems' 6 mm. Either the tabs move back 3 mm or the collars shrink to Ø 15.
- **Knobs:** CATHEDRAL's base part, **Tayda aluminium Ø 10 × 8, black**, with a white line. Octave knobs Ø 14 × 8 (part to pick: they must still clear the closed lid). **pressure in coral** (part open, common §2.2).
- **Strand tabs:** 6 mini rockers, 15 × 10, bone caps on dark bodies, in two columns of three (x 124.5 and 161.5; y 60 / 72 / 84). An ink bar marks the "on" half.
- **Gutter:** *low · main · high* printed once (y 60 / 72 / 84); TAP at y 101, a matte black cap in a bezel whose top face is lit (the status light).
- **HOLD:** a latching push button, Ø 9.2 cap on a Ø 12 bezel, with a coral dot.
- **Stays:** CATHEDRAL's brass flat bars at the 120° stand.

## Motion and feedback
- **Opening:**
  1. Lift the lid with one hand.
  2. Past ≈ 20° it's on: TAP's ring goes steady green.
  3. At 120° the stays click into their stop (CATHEDRAL's feel), and the cloth faces you.
- **Closing while playing:** the sound fades in ≈ 0.5 s (systems) as the lid comes down, so it never cuts off with a click. The ring goes out.
- **Strike / lean / lift:** no lights, by design. You hear each note bloom (soft) or bite (hard), swell as you lean, and fade as slowly as you lift. The felt collar gives the lean something to press into.
- **Strand tabs:** a short snap. A column's on-pattern reads like a small score: low and main on the left is cello-like; main and high on the right is violins.
- **HOLD:** a deep latching click; the cap sits lower and the coral dot lights.

## Push, reshape, risks
- **Push:** BOW is the most complete of the three, and its lid is the strongest form idea we have. I'd lead with it.
- **Reshape, for the director: is coral on the right control?** pressure is a selector you *set*. You rarely turn it while playing. Marking the thing you set as "where the life lives" is a little indirect.
  - **The alternative: coral felt collars under all 25 keys.** The life lives in the fingers, so the colour sits where the fingers are, and the knobs stay black. It's bolder and more literal, and it might be too loud.
  - I've drawn pressure. The director can judge it from words, or I'll draw the collars if wanted.
- **Risk: the key colours.** Dark naturals with pale sharps is the reversed reading the owner was asked about on Big Energy and hasn't answered. If he dislikes it, BOW falls back to normal colours (bone naturals, dark sharps), still on felt, and loses one of its signs of difference from KEPT.
- **Risk: the lap.** A lid standing at 120° with a speaker box in it puts weight behind the hinge. Eng to check the centre of mass. The visual fallback is a lower stand angle, or the lid flat (sound fires up).
- **Open:**
  - the octave knob part (Ø 14 × 8);
  - the coral part;
  - bone sharps: a printed cap or pale wood;
  - whether the lid's cloth can be a bought speaker grille fabric stretched on a printed frame (I assume yes; eng to confirm the acoustic loss).
