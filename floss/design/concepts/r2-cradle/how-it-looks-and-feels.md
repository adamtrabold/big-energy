## The idea, as you see it
The lid sits between its own cheeks. From the end you see one soft wedge, low at the front and 1.5T tall at the back. Flat, the slab lies inside the cheeks. Tilted, the plate comes up flush with the cheek tops. **The outline is the same in both states**, so the change happens inside a shape that doesn't change. That's what makes it distinctive, and it passes the thumbnail test (sheet view 7). SILL and PLINTH (flat) are both plain rounded blocks.

## Feel
- **Flat:** a low slab held by two raised ears at the back, like a lectern or a cheval mirror at rest.
- **Tilting:** lift the back, push it away, set it down. It lands on the rail with a dull, seated knock, not a click. The wrist edge doesn't move.
- **Playing:** the plate and cheek tops make one plane, so it looks like a single wedge of an instrument, not a slab on a stand.
- **In the bag:** a wedge 54 at its tallest. The ears guard the rear knob ends.
