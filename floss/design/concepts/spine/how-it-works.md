**The idea:** a clamshell (solander) box: two trays of one outline, one shallow and one deep, joined by a spine, closing square. The bookbinder's answer to "one thin cover and one fat cover is not a book" (the earlier director's addendum). **It's now printed, not bound** (14:08): the solander survives as the form; its making is printing plus a steel hinge rod.
- the **keyboard tray** (shallow);
- the **module tray** (deep);
- one **spine** with two hinges.

It's the rival to FALLBOARD: the smallest rigid kit in the bag.

**What sets the outline (director R6.5):**
- **412** = the family bed (404 inside) + walls.

- **Packed:** face down over the keyboard, panel to keys. The knobs sit under the module's rim, so nothing presses a key.

## 3. The spine: a geometric stop at flat (director R6; no trial)
**How it works [calc, PROPOSAL]:**
- Each hinge's axis sits **5 mm above the bottom face**, at the tray's spine edge. On the keyboard tray that's the top of its 5 mm ribbed floor; on the module tray it's the top of its 5 mm floor-edge beam.
- The spine panel carries a **5 mm bead** along each edge.
- **At 180°, each bead's face meets the tray's 5 mm edge face, full length (412 mm), below the axis.** A tray can't drop past flat: the load goes **face on face in compression**, and the hinge pin only hinges.
- **Folding toward closed is free:** the faces part, the trays swing up, and at 90° each the spine stands 2–5 mm outside the trays' edges and closes the back of the box [calc: rotating the bead about the axis keeps it clear of the floor edge].

**What it holds, and what it doesn't:**
- **Holds:** any load that would drop a tray's far edge below flat relative to the spine. That's a keypress, or the module tray hanging off your knees, with the spine and trays on your thighs or a bench.
- **Doesn't:** "bridged", meaning supported only at the front and back edges and pressed in the middle. That's the closing direction, so the joints fold. **Don't play it bridged** (as rev 1).
- **Load check [calc]:** a 20 N press at 143 mm is ≈ 2.9 N·m, which is ≈ 570 N across a 5 mm face. Spread over 412 mm of face and ≈ 6 knuckles per hinge, that's ≈ 95 N per knuckle. **Eng confirms: fine,** > 15× margin (≥ 4× under a whole palm) with knuckle OD ≥ 9, length ≥ 20 and a printed Ø 3.3 hole for the Ø 3 rod [r3 §0].
- **The trial step is gone** (PRINCIPLES §7: Adam measures nothing).

**Why 5 mm, not more:** each mm of stop face adds a mm to the closed box. 5 gives ≈ 570 N; 3 would be ≈ 950 N on the same knuckles. ENG can move it.

| State | Angle | For |
|---|---|---|
| **Closed** | 0° | carry; a bought webbing strap round the box |
| **Open flat** | 180°, **stops** | a lap, bench or table |

- **"Standing" (rev 1's ≈ 110°) is cut** (fewer notes). Two hinges don't hold an angle without a brace and a second stop, and flat already serves the bench, table and lap with the panel facing up. One line for the owner: "a standing pose was considered and dropped."
- **The lamp is cut** (director R6.1).

## 4. Cable path
- **Open:** the keyboard's rear edge faces the spine. The keyboard tray has no wall on that side, so **the space behind the keyboard runs straight into the 49 mm spine gap.** A straight plug (25–35 [r2]) from any rear socket fits, even behind an Xkey (0 + 49).
  - The plug passes over the 5 mm floor edge: the keyboard sits on the floor at z 5, so its socket is higher still.
  - End sockets pass the end block's notch.
- The cable crosses the spine and drops into the module tray's 30 mm lane, which is also open toward the spine, then into the **host port on the module's front face**.
  - The kit cable (flat right-angle C-to-C, module §9 S3) or a straight one both work here.
  - **The adapter** sticks out ≈ 50–60 mm from the host port [est]: across the lane and into the spine gap when open. **Packed, it comes off** and lives in the left bay.
- It never crosses hands, speaker or panel.
- **Friend:** as FALLBOARD (0.5 m beside you; optional coiled cable).
- **Packed:** the flat cable stays in the host port, its tail along the lane, the coil and the adapter in the left bay.
- You carry the closed box by its strap.

## 6. Flows
**Unpack and play:**
1. Lay the box down, spine away from you. Undo the strap.
2. Swing the module tray over to flat. It stops there.
3. Take the cable's free end from the left bay and plug in the keyboard (adapter if needed).
4. Switch on at the module's rear facet, through the far wall's window.
5. Play.

**Pack:**
1. Switch off; unplug the keyboard end (and the adapter, if used).
2. Tail along the lane; coil and adapter into the left bay.
3. Blocks locked.
4. Close the module tray over the keyboard.
5. Strap. Into the backpack.

**Keyboard missing:** close it empty, blocks locked. **Swap:** as FALLBOARD (against the near wall). **Charge:** the charger from the right bay into the rear USB-C through the window. **Home:** two thumbscrews.

## 9. Owner objections and trade-offs, up front
1. **Size:** closed 412 × 148 × 59, ≈ 3.6 L [calc], like a thick hardback a little wider than a keyboard. The smallest rigid kit. It stands in a daypack's main compartment (≥ ≈ 420 tall inside; UNVERIFIED).
2. **Weight:** ≈ 0.42–0.52 kg for the carry: printed is heavier than board and cloth. Reported (T5 withdrawn).
3. **Craft:** printing only (§8).
4. **No standing pose**, flat only (§3).
5. **Bridged = folds** (§3).
6. **Speaker:** sealed, published [r2].

## 10. Family reuse (job 28)
- **Unchanged:** the keyboard tray (bed, blocks, scale), the spine and its stop, the rods, the strap, the thumbscrews.
- **Per instrument:** the module tray's height (the module's height + 1.2) and its bays. A module deeper than ≈ 135 needs a deeper box: both trays grow together, so the box still closes square. The spine's width follows the closed thickness.
