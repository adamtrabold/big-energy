## 1. Point of view
**A solander box, printed.** Two walnut-brown trays of one outline and a spine. Closed it's a plain brown book-sized box; open flat it's one surface, keys near you, the module's cream deck across the spine. The bookbinder's form survives without the bookbinding.

## 3. The stop at flat, as something you see (director R6)
The geometric stop is two 5 mm beads along the spine panel's edges that bear face-on-face on the trays' 5 mm edge faces below the hinge axis at 180°. **Seen on the closed box, the two beads and their knuckle rows read like a book's raised bands across its spine.** That's the only "decoration" on SPINE, and it's the mechanism. The section at 3 : 1 on sheet A shows the faces meeting (the coral line on the sheet is a drawing mark, not a colour on the object).

## 5. Size and weight
- Closed 412 × 148 × 59 (B knobs: 66), ≈ 3.6 L.
- Carry 0.42–0.52 kg (systems; heavier than rev 1's board-and-cloth); kit + nanoKEY2 1.37–1.73, + K-Board C 1.47–1.83, + Xkey 25 1.73–2.09 kg.

## 6. How it compares with YOKE (now a concept, steer 2 S1)
SPINE and YOKE both stack the module over the keyboard, keys toward the module. SPINE adds two trays and a spine around that stack: full protection on all sides, a home for the coil, adapter and charger, and a flat open pose on a lap. YOKE (systems rev 1) drops all of that: ≈ 0.25–0.35 kg lighter carry, slightly larger (≈ 3.9 vs 3.6 L, from its arm ribs and cap), the panel is capped but the keyboard's front and back edges face the bag (unless wrapped), and the charger travels loose (unless wrapped). **YOKE is SPINE with the box taken away**; the owner's words decide whether the box is worth its weight.

## 7. Why
- **One outline, one brown:** the solander rule solves the "mismatched covers" problem; printing it in the module's brown makes it read as the instrument's own box.
- **The bands are the stop:** the one visible feature is the mechanism that makes the lap work.
