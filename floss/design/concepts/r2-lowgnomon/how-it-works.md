## The flat test: a flush bar under the front edge — **no**
- **What it does [calc]:** to level the plate the body pivots on its rear heel, and the front-bottom edge rises ≈ 16 on a ≈ 236-wide bar.
- **How it reads:** the base climbs toward the front over a 16 mm gap, and **the vertical back leans back 9.5°**. In the end view it's a tilted wedge standing on its heel and a front stilt. **It does read as "a wedge propped on a stilt."**
- **It also breaks his own words:** "I don’t like the angle on the back when laying flat. Make the base and the back 90 degrees". Flat is exactly where the back would lean.
- **And the recess fights the dock:** the bar's recess (y ≈ 2–20) sits across the S9 entry (y 10–70), and the 16-tall bar needs knuckle pockets under the thinnest part of the slab.
- **Verdict: drop flat.** LOW GNOMON is *slightly angled by shape, zero moving parts*. If he still wants flat-and-slight, SILL is that answer, already on the sheet.
