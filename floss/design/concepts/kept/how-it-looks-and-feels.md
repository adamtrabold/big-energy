## The idea
**Life lives in 1974.** It's ONE SHEET's single printed cream shell, laid out the way a string machine reads: registers first, then the ensemble, then out. White and black keys. Nothing on the face looks clever, because everything new (velocity, pressure, lift, the movable split) is under the fingers. It asks the owner whether he wants floss to *look* new or *feel* new.

**Draws on:** KEPT (director, turn 3); the Solina's own layout and its bass-only-on-the-left registers (director's fact check); ONE SHEET ("if it turns, it ripples"; one filament, one swap); and STOPS merged in (turns 2–3).

## Principles
1. **It looks like a string machine.** *Because* this face answers "feel new", so the panel can be one a 1974 player already knows.
2. **If it turns, it ripples.** *Because* ONE SHEET's rule costs nothing: concentric first-layer rings round every rotary, smooth everywhere else (tabs, HOLD, TAP, keys).
3. **The bass belongs to the left.** *Because* bass only sounds on the left half. Its tab sits beside OCTAVE L, and a sunk groove runs under the pair. OCTAVE R has its own short groove.
4. **Coral is the ensemble.** *Because* the bucket-brigade ensemble is what made 1974 strings sound like strings.
5. **Ordinary keys.** *Because* nothing on the face should look clever. It's the only concept that looks like a keyboard.

## Look
- **Shell:** cream PETG (#e8dcc2), printed face down on smooth PEI, one filament, one swap for the dark legends. 276 × **148** × 24 (systems' floor was 140; +8 so the grille clears the rear row's words).
- **Rings:** first-layer concentric rings round every rotary (ONE SHEET r5 values: ≈ 2 mm of ripple shows past each skirt).
- **Grooves:** sunk, 0.6 × 1.2. One under OCTAVE L and bass (x 12–52, y 55), one under OCTAVE R (x 246–264). They're the only section marks, and they mean "this belongs to that half".
- **Keys:** ONE SHEET's caps, white acrylic naturals 16.6 × 24 standing 2.5 proud at the front edge (edge A), and black sharps 11 × 19 standing 4.5 proud. They're two staggered rows, as on any Choc keyboard.
- **Knobs:** cream KN1250 Ø 14.9 with a dark line; octaves the same part, at the ends. **depth in coral** (part open).
- **Tabs:** 3 mini rockers, 15 × 10, cream caps (*bass · strings · brass*), with an ink bar on the "on" half.
- **TAP:** a black cap in a lit bezel (the status light), on the axis, front row.
- **Grille:** dark slots 1.6 wide at a 2.8 pitch, x 98–178, y 124–140, across the back band (ONE SHEET's language). No motif.

## The panel (real size: `mock-B-real-size.png`)
| | Left region | Gutter | Right region |
|---|---|---|---|
| rear y 100 | attack · release · pressure · (blank) | — | ensemble · time · tempo · repeats |
| front y 68 | octave · **bass** · strings · brass · hold | tap | **depth** · echo · reverb · volume · octave |

**Changes to systems' map:**
- attack and release move to the rear: the front was 5 + 5 dense, and they're ranges, not amounts.
- ensemble sits directly behind depth, so kind is behind amount.
- volume moves to the front; the blank moves to rear-left.
- strings and brass act on both halves but sit in the left region, as the Solina's register buttons did, next to the bass they extend. The groove under OCTAVE L and bass, and not under strings and brass, says which control is the left half's own.
- **Open:** whether that's clear enough cold. The alternative is strings and brass in the right region, which splits the registers across the axis, and that reads worse to me.

## Motion and feedback
- **Rear switch on:** TAP's ring goes steady. There's no lid and no ritual; it's an appliance, which is the point.
- **Tabs:** a short snap. bass on alone makes the right half silent; the tabs show why.
- **Strike:** louder when harder (systems §2.2): the meaning everyone already has.
- **HOLD:** a deep latching click; the cap sits lower and the coral dot lights.

## Push, reshape, risks
- **Keep it**, as the director framed it: the conventional answer, allowed to win. It's the slimmest, cheapest and simplest body.
- **An honest visual weakness:** ONE SHEET's keys are two staggered rows of Choc caps. They read as "a modern mini keyboard", not as 1974 keys. So KEPT's face is 1974 in its layout only. If the owner wants the 1974 reading stronger, the cheapest move is **sharps that sit between the naturals in plan** (a real keyboard silhouette, longer naturals). That's a key-strip change, eng's call, and it breaks the family's shared strip. I'm not recommending it; I'm naming it.
- **Risk:** it may read as "a Solina clone with velocity". The answer has to be in the hands, so the owner must play it, or see a video of strike / lean / lift, to judge it fairly.
- **Open:**
  - whether strings and brass in the left region read as "both halves" cold;
  - the coral part;
  - the sleeve (as Big Energy's ONE SHEET, deferred there).
