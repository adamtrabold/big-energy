# CLOTH: how it works (systems)

Status: **rev 2, 2026-11-22**, product designer (systems focus), concept round 2, second systems turn. Brought to the director's R6 (the near-black family cloth, the pose on the ground, FALLBOARD's footprint) and to the owner's 14:08 words: **"Cloth wrapping is fine as long as the core is printed."** The core here is the printed module; CLOTH has no other rigid part. The one module (280 × 105 × 35, hull) is in `../module/how-it-works.md`; this file covers only the carry. Marks: **[r2]** · **[calc]** · **PROPOSAL** · **UNVERIFIED**.

**The idea:** two objects in a soft frame, unpacked in a fixed order like a tea set. The module and keyboard travel wrapped in one quilted square cloth (a furoshiki, the Japanese wrapping cloth). Spread out, it's the playing surface and the frame: it owns the outline and hosts the keys.
- **On the ground it's the park kit**: the cloth is the mat you sit on and the bed the instrument sits on (§5). That's where it beats the rigid kits.
- **Honest label:** on a bench or the ground it frames the pair; on a lap it's two objects.

- **No home-octave sentence stitched on the cloth** (director R4). It's on the 1:1 card in the pocket and on the site.
- **What sets the outline:** FALLBOARD's 412 × 265, so the family footprint is one number. It isn't a multiple of 35 (FALLBOARD §1).

**Where the module sits, and why:**
- **Spread:** behind the keyboard on the outline, centred: the same relation as the rigid kits, so the mapping and the cable path are the same.
- **Packed:**
  1. module in its sleeve at the bottom, panel up;
  2. the keyboard keys-down on the sleeve's quilted top;
  3. the cloth knotted over both.
- **Why this order:** the keys bear only the keyboard's own weight (0.24–0.6 kg [eng]), on quilt over the module's flat rim. The knobs sit under the rim.

**Every area of the spread cloth:** the outline holds the module and keyboard; the near cloth is the seat; the pocket corner holds what travels with the cable; the rest is the knot's corners. Nothing else lives on it.

## 2. Keyboard range (job 3, 27)
- **Every keyboard, chunky minis included:** the only kit that carries them (owner trade-off).
- **No end blocks:** it holds by wrapping. **Nothing crosses a key:** the keys face the quilted sleeve, never a band.
- **Any socket position:** nothing hems the keyboard in, so a straight plug, the flat kit cable or the adapter all fit.

## 3. Cable path
- **Spread:** the 0.3–0.5 m cable [r2] runs a few centimetres across the cloth from the keyboard's socket (rear or end) to the host port on the module's front face. It never crosses hands, speaker or panel.
- **Friend:** the keyboard goes beside you (0.5 m) or onto a friend's lap on the optional coiled cable.
- **Packed:** cable and adapter in the pocket.

## 4. The split target (job 6)
On the module (module §4): the printed target over B | C. The card in the pocket explains the home octave.

## 5. Flows
**Unpack on a bench or table (the ritual):**
1. Untie the knot.
2. Spread the cloth, outline facing you.
3. Lift off the keyboard (it's on top) onto the outline's front half.
4. Slip the module from its sleeve onto the back half, on the dashed line.
5. Cable (and adapter) from the pocket: keyboard → host port.
6. Switch on.
7. Play.

**On the ground (the park pose; director R6, owner 2026-11-13: "take this to the park"), PROPOSAL:**
1. Choose a flat patch of grass. Untie and spread the cloth, **coated side down** (it keeps damp out), with the outline on the far side.
2. **Sit on the near cloth**, cross-legged or kneeling. The ≈ 435 mm in front of the outline is your seat, so you aren't on wet grass.
3. Keyboard on the outline's front half, module behind on the dashed line, as on a bench.
4. Cable from the pocket (at your near corner, within reach); switch on.
5. Play, leaning in. **The speaker fires up through the crown** [r2: fires up], so grass and cloth don't muffle it.
6. **Or:** keyboard on your lap, module on the outline in front of you, on the cable (two objects, which is fine on the ground).
7. **Wind:** the corner weights hold the cloth.
8. **Pack:** stand up, the reverse ritual, and shake off the coated side before knotting.

**On a lap (honest):** the cloth drapes and stops being a frame. **The lap pose is two objects:** the keyboard on your lap, the module on the bench beside you, on the cable.

**Keyboard missing:** the module alone in its sleeve, knotted: the smallest bundle. **Swap:** wrap whatever keyboard you have; re-set the target. **Home:** nothing to undo.

## 8. Owner objections and trade-offs, up front
1. **Size:** the module alone goes in a laptop sleeve. With a keyboard, the bundle is keyboard-sized and soft.
2. **Weight:** the lightest kit (reported; T5 withdrawn).
3. **Craft:** sewing, around the printed module (owner 14:08: fine).
4. **Protection:** soft only.
5. **At night:** only the module's TAP ring and port light. Honest.
6. **Speaker:** sealed, published [r2].

## 9. Family reuse (job 28)
- **Unchanged:** the cloth, the outline (the family's 412 × 265), the pocket and the weights.
- **Per instrument:** the sleeve (its size). The cloth knots round a module up to ≈ 450 long [calc].
