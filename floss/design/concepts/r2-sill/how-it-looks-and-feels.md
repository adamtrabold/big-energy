## The idea, as you see it
CATHEDRAL's lid lying down: GNOMON rev 5's slab, sitting flat.

A single full-width printed bar lies flush in the rear of the base. Flip it down with a fingertip and the back lifts slightly. From above you never see the bar. The panel is the same in both states; only the slope changes.

## Why the shape (not costume)
- **It's the shape he already liked:** CATHEDRAL's lid, a calm framed slab with soft architectural corners. "Flat or slightly angled" needs nothing more than that slab and one line of support under its back.
- **The bar is the sill:** a plain, full-width line, the window-sill under the frame. Folded, it's flush with the base, so the slab keeps its unbroken soft outline in the bag and on the table.
- **≈ 9.2° reads as deliberately tilted, not as a drafting error** (the director's point). It's still "slight", and it comes from the slab's own number (half its thickness).

## Feel
- **Flat:** a quiet slab you can put anywhere: table, lap or grass.
- **Tilting:** a fingertip in the notch, the bar swings down and clicks into its stop. One move, and it never needs adjusting.
- **Playing:** a low desk angle, your wrist on the soft front edge. From the player's side it looks like the lid simply rests at a slight lean.
- **In the bag:** the smallest object of the loop, 280 × 115 × 35, with nothing sticking out.

## CHOCK (inset): an honest note for systems
The director asked for "a loose full-width wedge stored in the same recess". **A true wedge 17.5 tall won't fit a 3.5 mm recess**, and a recess deep enough for one would take ≈ 50 cm³ from E's box [calc, rough]. So I drew CHOCK as a **loose tapered strip** (≈ 17.5 × 6 at the foot × 280). It lies flat in the bar's recess and stands on edge in a shallow groove under the back.

That makes it functionally a bar with no hinge. Its trade, unchanged: **no moving part, but it can be lost** (then floss plays flat). **Systems: please confirm the groove, or say whether CHOCK means something else.**
