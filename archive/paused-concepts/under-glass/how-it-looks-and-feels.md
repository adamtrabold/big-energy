# UNDER GLASS: how it looks and feels (visual, round 2)

Kind: **an exposure**. Pole: middle. Body: **sleeve-carried**. The director's replacement for ONE SHEET. The owner hasn't seen it yet, and his three asks came first this round. Read with `how-it-works.md` (systems) and `../common-looks-and-feels.md`.

Mocks: `mock-A-look.png` (3/4 view at a playing-eye 46° + in a sleeve), `mock-B-real-size.png`, `mock-C-edge-section.png`.

## The idea
A pinball machine keeps its mechanism under glass with the art painted on the back. The printed board is the playfield; a reverse-painted clear sheet is the glass, with windows onto the voice.

## Principles
1. **The board is the playfield.** *Because* he chose a printed board: the circuit is worth seeing, so the case frames it, as a pinball frames its playfield.
2. **Windows only where the voice is.** *Because* a playfield shows what's in play: the three VCO cores and the ladder. The rest is painted out; the two lights shine through unpainted dots.
3. **The art is under the glass.** *Because* paint behind 3 mm of acrylic can't be worn by hands: legends are engraved mirrored into the underside and back-filled first, then his paint goes over them. That is H6 as a painting order.
4. **Rails, not cheeks.** *Because* a lockdown bar is where the hands rest: bought aluminium channel front and back, smoked acrylic ends.
5. **The circuit is the only colour.** *Because* it's the specimen: everything else is black, white and bare metal. The red film caps in the ladder window are the colour.

## Look
- **Glass:**
  - gloss black ground (his paint, behind 3 mm of clear);
  - white back-filled legends, scales and MIDI dots;
  - section titles over a thin white rule behind the back row;
  - octave marks C1–C3 in the key-edge band.
- **Windows** (y 80–96):
  - each framed by a thin painted white rule;
  - a museum-style caption in italic serif under each: "fig. 1 three oscillators", "fig. 2 the ladder". The serif is a typeset stand-in for his lettering.
  - Through them, on a black-mask board with white silkscreen and gold pads:
    - three AS3046 DIP-14 with a green tempco bead bonded on each;
    - the ladder's TO-92 pairs, and **four red film caps at the back of the window**, where the eye sees them past the window's front edge.
- **Labels by the windows:** front-row knobs over a window (RANGE, WAVE, CUTOFF, EMPHASIS, AMOUNT) have their labels in a strip just in front of the window (y 76.6), so no label sits over the parts.
- **Edge:** the glass's polished front edge is a line of light over the switch backs: the key edge.
- **Metal:** a 1 in rear rail (it sets the 25.4 body) and a 1/2 in front rail below the keys, bare aluminium; smoked acrylic ends with the jacks in the right end.
- **Knobs: white KN1250** (director: black or white; no black KN1250 exists), cream CUTOFF with a red line. Keys white and black acrylic.
- **Status light:** amber through an unpainted dot. "Not in control": an LED on the board, seen *inside* the ladder window by CUTOFF.

## Feel
- One smooth glass sheet: **no tactile section breaks.** It's the set's weakest on blind finding (J16); the knobs and toggles are the only marks.
- The rails are the hand rests.

## Reactions to systems / eng
- **Speaker moved to (150, 150):** the common (170, 128) is ≈ 8 mm from the ladder window. Eng wants ≥ 30 mm from the ladder and expos; at (150, 150) it's ≈ 36 mm from both windows and 73 mm from the sensors.

- **Systems' "black KN1250" doesn't exist** → white.

## Open questions
- **Parallax (Q-UG2, and a bigger one):** the parts sit 7–10 mm under the glass top. From a low angle (≈ 34°), the painted band in front hides most of a 16 mm window; from a playing eye (≈ 45–60°) you see the back two-thirds, which is why the red caps moved to the back row. If the owner likes the idea:
  - deepen the windows front-to-back (y 78–100), or
  - put the tallest, most colourful parts at the back of each window.
- Legend size under 3 mm of acrylic: 1.9 mm reads in the mock top view; a test engraving will tell.
- Board mask colour (owner): black is assumed. Green would make the windows read more "circuit", less "specimen".
