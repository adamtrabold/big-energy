# Naming round: the budget instrument (design director)

**Owner (verbatim):** "little D is a terrible name. It sounds like little penis. I could see big D energy or just big energy or something like that, but the name needs work." The owner also says this is now "the real instrument… the most interesting thing overall".

## The brief, from the idea of the thing

- **What it is:** a three-oscillator, ladder-filter mono synth with a speaker.
  It's the Model D's own move, a reduction made portable, made again at
  budget size. It's hand-built by its owner, in a box, and played for bedroom
  pop.
- **The tension the name has to carry:** it's small and cheap, but it
  **sounds big**. The owner's "big energy" is exactly this: a size inversion.
- **Rules:**
  - No "D", "Mini", "Moog" or "Model". The name has to work without the parent.
  - Nothing that's a joke about smallness. The flaw of "Little D" was
    calling attention to size the wrong way.
  - It must read on a panel and a hand-lettered label: short, with good letter
    shapes and no awkward ligatures.
  - It must say well out loud ("I played it on the ___").

**Real draws** (`secrets.SystemRandom`):
- **Fields:** radio, birds, sports, astronomy.
- **Oblique Strategy:** "Emphasize the flaws".
- **Twists:** "a colour", "a number in it", "a verb, not a noun".

## The names (11, in five directions)

### A. Big energy: the owner's direction, made into a word

**1. WALLOP** ★ *top 3*
- **Pronounced:** WOLL-up.
- **Why:** "packs a wallop" means a small thing that hits hard. That's the
  owner's "big energy" without the meme. It's a **verb** (draw), so it describes
  what the instrument does, not what it is. It sounds like the ladder filter
  opening with the emphasis up.
- **Panel:** six letters, round and blunt, with the W and the double L good
  for hand lettering. It works in lowercase too ("wallop").
- **Risks:** a little comic. That suits the owner's register in this project,
  but test it read cold. **Check:** no synth or pedal called Wallop turned up in
  a quick search (results only showed unrelated pedal listings; see the
  sources). A full trademark search is still needed before he commits.

**2. BIG ENERGY**
- **Pronounced:** as written.
- **Why:** the owner's own words, kept literal.
- **Panel:** two words is long for a wordmark. It works as a stacked lockup.
- **Risks:** it reads like an energy drink or a meme, and it'll date fast. If
  the owner wants it, "BIG E" is a shorter panel mark, but it loses the joke.
  I'm listing it as his anchor, not as a pick.

**3. BDE**
- **Why:** the honest version of what he said.
- **Risks:** it's the "little penis" joke flipped, and it stays a joke about
  the same thing. It fails my "no smallness joke" rule in reverse. I'm
  recording it so it's been considered. **Not recommended.**

### B. Small bird, big voice (birds draw)

**4. WREN** ★ *top 3*
- **Pronounced:** REN.
- **Why:** the wren is one of the smallest songbirds and one of the
  loudest for its size. The Carolina wren is common across the eastern US
  and sings far louder than it looks. **Size inversion as a fact of nature**,
  not a boast. It also suits a quiet, warm, felt-and-wood object (HUSH,
  HYMNAL) and bedroom pop.
- **Panel:** four letters, very clean, with a beautiful W for lettering. A
  small bird glyph could be the one ornament he draws.
- **Risks:** there's an existing **Wren**: a free, open-source *software* modular
  synthesizer by Blue Hell, listed on AlternativeTo and documented on the
  Blue Hell wiki (see the sources). It's small and non-commercial, but it is
  a synth. That's fine for a personal instrument. If he ever sells the kit,
  it needs a trademark check, or a variant ("Carolina", "Jenny Wren").

**5. BANTAM**
- **Pronounced:** BAN-tum.
- **Why:** birds and sports draws converge: a bantam chicken and a
  bantamweight are both small fighters with big punches.
- **Panel:** good letter rhythm.
- **Risks:** there's a **Bantam (prototype)** eurorack module listed on
  ModularGrid, and Bantam Tools is an established desktop CNC brand
  (see the sources). It's crowded.

### C. Small transmitter, long reach (radio draw)

**6. SKYWAVE**
- **Why:** skywave is a radio signal that bounces off the ionosphere, so a
  small transmitter can be heard a continent away. That's big energy as
  physics.
- **Risks:** it's already used by consumer radio products and satellite
  companies (not checked in this round). It's also long for a panel.

**7. CARRIER**
- **Why:** the carrier is the wave the music rides on.
- **Risks:** it's dry and too engineering-flavoured for this owner.
  **Weakest of the set.**

### D. Things that holler (verb draw, the folk and cigar-box line)

**8. HOLLER** ★ *top 3*
- **Pronounced:** HOLL-er.
- **Why:**
  - A holler is a big voice from one person with nothing but lungs. The
    **field holler** is the root of American vernacular music, which is the
    same cheap-instrument, play-it-yourself tradition as the cigar-box
    guitar behind HYMNAL.
  - It's a **verb** (draw): it says what the instrument does.
  - In the Appalachian South a "holler" is also a small valley. A small
    place that carries sound is the idea of the object again. A mono synth
    *is* one voice hollering.
- **Panel:** six letters with doubled Ls, which hand-letter beautifully, and
  it suits the HYMNAL label and a felt or wood body.
- **Risks:** it could read as regional or rustic to some people. That's a
  fit for this owner and his materials, not a problem, but say it out loud
  first. **Check:** no synth, module or pedal named Holler turned up in a
  search; the results were generic eurorack listings (see the sources).
  A full trademark search is still needed.

**9. BELT**
- **Why:** to belt a song is to sing it big.
- **Risks:** it reads as clothing first. **Weak.**

### E. Emphasize the flaws, and the colour and number draws

**10. RED GIANT**
- **Why:** a red giant is a star that's swollen huge while running cool and
  cheap (astronomy + colour draws). The panel's signal colour gives it a
  ready mark.
- **Risks:** **Red Giant** is an established video-effects software brand.
  Avoid.

**11. THREE-O (3°)**
- **Why:** three oscillators (number draw). Reading it as "three degrees",
  the slightly-out-of-tune warmth of analog oscillators, is the
  "emphasize the flaws" draw: the drift is the sound.
- **Panel:** a superscript ° is a lovely glyph.
- **Risks:** you can't say it out loud without explaining it.

## Top 3

1. **HOLLER.** This is the best fit for the idea of the thing: one voice
   carrying further than its size, from the cheap-instrument folk tradition
   the concepts already belong to. No collisions were found in this search.
2. **WREN.** The quietest and most beautiful of the three, with size
   inversion as a fact of nature. There's one small software-synth
   collision, which is fine for a personal instrument.
3. **WALLOP.** The owner's "big energy" direction, turned into a single
   word that sounds like the filter. No collisions were found in this
   search.

**Nothing here is a full trademark clearance.** If he ever sells the kit
(he has floated it), run a USPTO search in class 15 (musical
instruments) before printing.
