## 1. What changes from ONE SHEET, and what doesn't
**Unchanged:** the printed shell's outline (276 × 180), heights, every control, the main board, the **I/O board on the printed rear wall with the jack barrels flush in its holes**, the speaker, the battery, the back panel, v1.1's grooves and type.

**Changes:**
1. **The joint moves to y 165.0** (§2).
2. **The rear band becomes a walnut top, laid on the shell like a tabletop on its apron** (§3).
3. **Two bookmatched leaves joined on the axis by one butterfly** (§4).
4. **A French-cleat slot** in the back panel, to hang (§5).
5. **FREE / SYNC** at (128.0, 132.7) in v1.1 ONE SHEET style, with the label fixes in common §2 (TIME / DECAY legends behind; MIXER heading to y 136).

## 2. Where the joint can go [CHECK]
- At the visual designer's **y 160, the joint cuts four type-fan names**: tape and ping-pong (ECHO TYPE), plate and shimmer (REVERB TYPE). Those names sit at y ≈ 159–160. The ECHO / REVERB headings (baseline 161.8, tops 163.4) would land on the wood just behind.
- **The joint must sit at y ≥ 164.9 → y 165.0.** That's 11.5 behind the FX knob edges (153.45) and 1.5 behind the headings' tops. Every legend stays on the print.
- Wood from the joint to the printed rear wall: **15.0 mm** at the deepest notch of the live edge (the director asked for ≈ 20; 15 is what the legends leave).

## 3. Construction: the top and the apron (why the jacks aren't drilled through wood)
**Drilling the jacks through the wood fails** [CALC]:
- the rail is 15 mm deep at its thinnest, and more where the edge wanders;
- a 3.5 mm plug's shaft is ≈ 14–15 mm long (ESTIMATE), so plugs wouldn't reach a jack mounted on the rail's inside face;
- moulded plug bodies need bores ≈ 8–10 mm wide (ESTIMATE);
- the live face is irregular, so no I/O board can sit flush behind it.

**What works, and it's honest furniture: a tabletop over an apron.**
- **The printed shell keeps its rear wall** (y 177.6–180) with the I/O board and the flush jacks exactly as v1.1.
- **Behind the joint, the shell's top steps down 6 mm** (a rebate, y 165–180, z 18–24).
- **The walnut leaves (≈ 6 mm thick) lie in the rebate, flush with the face** (z 24). Their live edge **overhangs the printed rear wall** by the edge's wander: **0 at the deepest notch, ≈ 10–12 at the peaks** (recommend ≤ 12). Overall depth **≈ 180–192**; the printed body stays 180.
- **The jacks sit under the overhang**, sheltered like a drawer under a tabletop; cables leave under the live edge. Jack centres z ≈ 8–12 vs the leaf underside z 18: clear.
- **Jack legends:** they were printed on the rear band (y 171); now they're **laser-engraved into the walnut**, located from the straight joint edge (one fence, no jig for a wandering edge).
- **Fixing:** screws from inside, up through the rebate floor into the leaves, in **holes slotted front to back** for wood movement. Across the grain the leaves are only ≈ 15–27 mm, so seasonal movement is ≲ 0.5 mm (ESTIMATE).
- **Lighter than it looks** (director): ≈ 6 mm of walnut, the shell hollow as before. Walnut ≈ 276 × 25 × 6 ≈ 41 cm³ ≈ 25–30 g (ESTIMATE).

## 4. The leaves and the butterfly
- **Grain runs along x**: a live edge always runs along the grain, so the two leaves meet **end to end at x 138**. That makes it a **butt (end) match**, not a side-by-side bookmatch.
  - Resaw one offcut into two leaves; flip the second end for end. Its live edge then mirrors the first about the axis (the director's "the wild edge mirrored about the centre").
- **End grain to end grain glues badly.** **The butterfly really is the joint**: one walnut-contrast key (wenge / bog oak), ≈ 20 × 10 on top, inlaid ≈ 3 mm (half the leaf), across x 138.
  - It holds wood to wood (the director's condition).
  - Nakashima's butterflies usually cross long-grain splits. **This use across an end butt is less traditional (precedent UNVERIFIED)**, though it does carry the joint.
- Each leaf ≈ 138 long: short offcuts, a small bench, cheap (ESTIMATE).

## 5. Hanging (J18, a real job here)
- **French-cleat slot** routed at 45° along the back panel's rear zone, **y ≈ 150–170, x ≈ 40–200**. It clears the speaker (x 215–265, y 88–138).
- Hung, the instrument's rear (the tree side) is up, the knobs face the room, and the keys are at the bottom.
- **Battery strap position on the back panel vs the slot: UNVERIFIED** (check at layout).
- The cleat carries ≈ 1 kg (ESTIMATE). A 3 mm printed back panel needs a doubler or a printed rib under the slot (maker).
