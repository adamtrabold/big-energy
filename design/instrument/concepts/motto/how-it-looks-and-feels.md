## The idea, as it should read

A tiny harpsichord. The bentside curve is the panel itself, the rose is the
speaker, and the lid stands up behind the panel facing the player like an
altarpiece: a raw-birch canvas that the owner paints. Reverse-colour keys
(walnut naturals, bone sharps).

## Looks

- **Body:** cork-faced layers; the outline: straight spine and tail section,
  then a 30 mm S-curve to the right end.
- **Panel:** white (Proof-like), dark legends, gold rules, chartreuse hero.
  Rose grille Ø40 at the rear-left: laser-cut petals over the driver. kbd 1/2
  sit beside the rose; the rest is the common layout, rows shifted 15 mm toward
  the keys so the curve has room.
- **Eleven amber pips (V-c):** one at the upper-right of each gold-ringed
  control (glide, tune, freq 2, freq 3, cutoff) and at the top of each fader
  slot. The mock shows two lit (cutoff, filter decay).
- **Keys:** K-B levers with CNC walnut tops and bone acrylic sharps.
- **Lid:** walnut ply outside only; inside raw birch with the ribbon
  "[ your motto here ]" and nothing else. Seen from the player, the lid's bottom
  edge follows the bentside. Brass piano hinge on the straight tail, brass stay
  at the right.

## Feel and motion, by state

| State | What you see / feel |
|---|---|
| closed | walnut lid on cork body: a recognisable outline, 46 thick |
| opening | lift the front edge; LED breathes from ≈ 20°; the stay clicks at 100° |
| playing | the painted face stands behind the panel; sound rises from the rose in front of it (reflector UNVERIFIED) |
| MIDI owns a control | that control's pip lights: "this one isn't in control; move it to take it back" |
| closing | lift slightly to release the stay; it folds into the lid; no half-open detent (can't fall on a hand) |
| lid removed for painting | 6 hinge screws + stay; plays with no lid |
| lights off | pointers, sharp stripes, lit pips (inset) |

## Why

- R4.1–R4.6 each shown: material read (cork + white), abstract placeholder
  (ribbon only), rose Ø40 stated + reflector "unverified" printed on the sheet,
  11 pips, finger dials stay cut, walnut-faced keys kept (now as service-cut
  tops on bought switches).
- Chartreuse is deliberately wrong-looking; it is one cap, so it costs nothing
  to swap if the owner hates it.
