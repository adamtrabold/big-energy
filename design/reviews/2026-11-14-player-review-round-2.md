# Player review, round 2: CATHEDRAL P5R re-laid, and three P5O studies

**Date:** 2026-11-14
**Reviewer:** independent critic, playing synths for decades (Model D, Prophet, Juno, Minibrute, Grandmother, OP-1, Make Noise and Mutable modules)
**Judged from:** the new P5R flat top view and its standing view, and the three P5O study top views (amp column, amp row, levels on the lid). I judged fresh and with my hands first.

**The bar:** the owner's 10, meaning "I'd play this happily on stage and in the studio, and I'd defend every control's place."

**Short version:**
- P5R took a real step. It's the first layout I'd play without grumbling, but it isn't a 10.
- None of the three P5O studies is there either.
- There **is** a version of the owner's own layout, with the amp envelope as the top row, that I'd defend, and it's close to the amp-row study. It's described at the end.

## What works now (keep it)

1. **The base's left side is finally right.** The PITCH and FILTER mod-destination switches sit beside the mod controls. BLEND is over MOD and RELEASE is over GLIDE, so the columns now line up as true pairs. The duplicate "mod off · on" is gone. This was the biggest player trap in round 1, and it's fixed.
2. **OUT is one group on the base: VOLUME, PAN and LINK.** The echo's PAN now lives inside ECHO on the lid. The "two pans, two outs" confusion is gone.
3. **Each switch now sits with what it changes.**
   - TRACK sits at the end of oscillator 3's row in P5R, and in oscillator 3's row in the studies.
   - VOICES, VELOCITY and TOUCH form one KEYS group.
   - VOICES says 1 · 3 · 4 everywhere.
4. **The knob markings follow one rule.** Every continuous knob on the lid has the same tick ring at the same weight. The arrows, triangles and stray long ticks are gone. That's the owner's "markings are all inconsistent" note answered.
5. **The type selectors read.** TAPE · DARK · CLEAN · PINGPONG and SPRING · PLATE · HALL · SHIMMER are printed as one line under each knob, left to right in turn order. Reverb now runs small to large. Echo TIME shows 16 · 8 · 8. · 4 · 2 · 1 for SYNC. A player can read all of it.
6. **Both type knobs come first in their groups** (type, then time; type, then decay).
7. **Filter TRACK reads off · ½ · full,** so self-oscillating the filter and playing it in tune now works.
8. **The "big energy" wordmark is gone.**
9. **The P5O studies' oscillator bank is the best oscillator block so far.** It has an even row pitch (about 25 mm), the numbers sit in a clear margin column (x ≈ 9 mm in from the bank), and TUNE and DRIFT fill oscillator 1's empty pitch slot, with TRACK 3 in oscillator 3's row.

## What still doesn't work, most important first

### P5R (lid 8, base 7)
1. **The oscillators are still strung across the full width.** Ten knobs plus a switch fill a 290 mm row, and oscillator 2's PITCH and oscillator 3's PITCH (the pair you detune against each other) sit about 46 mm apart. This is the owner's "the oscillators are really far apart", rotated. It's playable, but I'd argue for the stacked bank every time.
2. **In a single row, TUNE and DRIFT read as oscillator 1's own knobs.** In a stacked bank they sit in the pitch column, so they read as "the master pitch, at the reference oscillator." In a row they sit in "1 · octave · wave · tune · drift", which says "osc 1's tune." A first-time player will think TUNE tunes oscillator 1 alone.
3. **The oscillator numbers still hug the tick rings.** The "1", "2" and "3" sit about 1–2 mm from the outer ticks. The P5O studies fixed this with a margin column; P5R didn't.
4. **The FILTER and AMP headers sit on top of the DECAY label.** That puts two lines of text over one knob, and it reads like the knob is called "filter decay" while the other two knobs have no group. Put the header over the group's left edge, or above all three labels with more space.
5. **The ECHO header is jammed against the bottom of the grille** (about 2 mm clear). Echo's TYPE and TIME also start under the grille's left edge, so the group's centre isn't under its header.

### The base (7, all four)
6. **The OUT header is in the wrong place.** It sits on row 1 above the REVERB level, so "out" and "reverb" read as one group. VOLUME and PAN are on row 2. Put OUT over VOLUME · PAN on row 2, and move LINK beside PAN on that row, since PAN is the only thing it changes.
7. **PITCH off · on sits directly over the BEND stick.** A new player will read it as "bend on/off." It's really "mod goes to pitch." Either draw a thin printed bracket that ties PITCH and FILTER to MOD, or put the two switches over MOD and BLEND and move BLEND over BEND. Swapping keeps the pairs: BLEND (mod source) over BEND is the weaker pairing, so the bracket is my preference.
8. **The dead zones are still there.**
   - On row 2, GLIDE to RESONANCE is about 39 mm of nothing.
   - On row 1, RELEASE to MIXER 1 is about 30 mm, and SOURCE to the ECHO level is about 32 mm.
   - The mixer knobs sit 0–9 mm off the filter knobs below them, and ECHO and REVERB don't line up with TAP, VOLUME or PAN.

   The base still looks unplanned, which is the owner's "less senseful."
9. **TAP is still a brass dome** shaped like a natural key. The green ring helps, and I assume it's the tempo LED, which I like. But the cap should still be a different finish. Also, TEMPO over TAP is two labels for one button, which breaks the one-word rule. Use just TAP.

### Markings, follow-through (P5R and the studies)
10. **Stepped knobs use the continuous tick ring.** OCTAVE and the two TYPE knobs show the same evenly spaced ticks as WAVE. A detented knob should show one tick per detent, so your eye and your fingers agree. OCTAVE also has no position labels. Add the footages (32 · 16 · 8 · 4 · 2 · or whatever positions the firmware uses) in the same small one-line style as TIME.
11. **The MIDI dots disappeared.** The architecture says "a DAW can move any knob marked with a MIDI dot." If the dots are gone on purpose, the doc needs to change. If not, bring them back as one dot, always in the same place (for example just right of the label), on knobs only. The owner's workflow is DAW-first, so this matters more than it looks.

### The P5O studies
12. **Amp column (6):** this is the weakest of the three.
    - The bottom-left quadrant under oscillator 3 is empty (about 60 × 30 mm), and so is the lower right under REVERB.
    - KEYS floats at the top centre between the oscillators and ECHO.
    - FREE/SYNC sits at the far right, a full knob away from TIME.
    - The REVERB header collides with ECHO's REPEATS label.
13. **Amp row (7):** this is the right idea, and the closest to the owner's words. The AMP row is centred over the grille, and the FILTER column runs down the grille's left side. But:
    - FILTER SUSTAIN hangs alone at the bottom.
    - The two lower corner quadrants are empty again, so the panel is top-heavy.
    - KEYS as a vertical stack of three switches reads as a column of unrelated switches.
    - The REVERB/REPEATS collision is still there.
14. **Levels on the lid (5):** no. ECHO MIX and REVERB MIX are play controls: you throw the echo up at the end of a phrase. They belong a hand's width from the keys, which is where the base has them. As drawn, the study also **keeps the base's ECHO and REVERB levels**, so each level exists twice. Two knobs for one parameter can't be defended. And "mix" on the lid against "echo"/"reverb" on the base is two words for one thing.

## Is there a version of the owner's layout I'd defend? Yes.

His direction (20:43, confirmed 01:27): oscillators stacked on the left, the filter envelope in the middle around the speaker, and the amp group left to right along the lid's top row. The amp-row study is about 80% of it. The missing idea is to **make the filter envelope a row too, and put it at the bottom.** That gives the lid a centre spine: **AMP row, then the grille, then the FILTER row.**

**The lid, on a 4-row grid** (about 25 mm row pitch, the same 20 mm column pitch everywhere):

| | Left (oscillators) | Centre (envelopes + speaker) | Right (effects) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Row 1** | 1: octave · wave · tune · drift | **AMP** attack · decay · sustain | **ECHO** type · time · free/sync |
| **Row 2** | 2: octave · wave · pitch | grille | repeats · pan |
| **Row 3** | 3: octave · wave · pitch · track | grille | *(air, with the REVERB header)* |
| **Row 4** | **KEYS** voices · velocity · touch | **FILTER** attack · decay · sustain | **REVERB** type · decay |

**Why a player would defend each choice:**
- **Filter envelope at the bottom, amp at the top.** On a standing lid the bottom row is the closest reach. FILTER DECAY is the envelope knob you ride mid-phrase, and the amp envelope is mostly set per patch. The owner's top-row amp turns out to be the right place for a player too.
- **Both envelopes are rows of A · D · S in the same order,** one above and one below the speaker. You learn one, and you know the other. That pairing is the "envelopes label" problem solved by layout instead of by a header on the grille.
- **The bottom row is full** (keys, filter, reverb), and so is the top (osc 1, amp, echo). The empty corners of all three studies disappear.
- **The one gap left is deliberate.** The space on the right at row 3 separates ECHO from REVERB and gives the REVERB header room, which fixes the collision.
- **KEYS under the oscillators makes sense.** VOICES decides how notes go to those oscillators, and VELOCITY and TOUCH are how the keyboard plays them. Three switches sit in the octave · wave · pitch columns, so they line up.
- **FREE/SYNC sits right beside TIME,** the knob whose meaning it changes.
- **Everything the base needs stays on the base:** levels, cutoff, mixer, out and tap. The rule of settings on the lid and play controls on the base holds without exception.

**Check before committing:** the AMP and FILTER rows are three knobs at 20 mm (about 54 mm across with labels). The grille (about 45 mm) has to fit in rows 2–3 between them with a clear margin, about 50 mm of height. At a 25 mm row pitch that is tight but plausible. If it doesn't fit, shrink the grille a little before you touch the row pitch. The owner asked for the best sound from the chosen speaker, so check the grille's open area with eng.

Done cleanly, with the marking fixes below, I'd defend this at 9 going on 10. I'd take it over P5R, because the oscillator bank and the paired envelopes are worth more to a player than P5R's tidy rows.

## Suggestions (numbered, in order of payoff)

1. **Draw the "centre spine" P5O** above (amp row, grille, filter row; keys under the oscillators; reverb on the bottom right) and show it beside P5R. It's the owner's own layout, done in a way a player can defend.
2. **Fix the base's OUT group:** put the OUT header over VOLUME · PAN on row 2, with LINK on that row beside PAN.
3. **Tie the mod destinations to MOD:** a thin printed bracket from MOD to PITCH and FILTER, so PITCH no longer reads as "bend on/off."
4. **Put the base on a grid:**
   - Line up the mixer over the filter row, and the effect levels over TAP, VOLUME and PAN.
   - Close the 39 mm and 30 mm gaps by pulling the groups together and centring the result.
5. **Finish the marking rule:**
   - Stepped knobs (OCTAVE, both TYPE knobs) get one tick per detent.
   - OCTAVE gets one-line position labels like TIME's.
   - Bring back one MIDI dot per MIDI-controllable knob, always in the same spot, or change the architecture doc.
6. **TAP:** give it a non-brass cap, keep the ring, and use one label (TAP).
7. **If P5R stays in the running:**
   - Move the oscillator numbers into a margin like the studies.
   - Split TUNE and DRIFT from oscillator 1 with a small gap or a TUNING label, so they read as global.
   - Lift the FILTER and AMP headers off the DECAY label.
   - Give ECHO's header clearance from the grille.
8. **Drop P5OL.** Effect levels stay on the base, and the lid never duplicates a base control.

**Where I stand:** P5R is good. The centre-spine P5O, as described, is the one I'd argue *for*. Show me that render and I'll judge it fresh.
