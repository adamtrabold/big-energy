# Player review: CATHEDRAL layouts P5R and P5O (devil's advocate)

**Date:** 2026-11-14
**Reviewer:** independent critic, playing synths for decades (Model D, Prophet, Juno, Minibrute, Grandmother, OP-1, Make Noise and Mutable modules)
**Judged from:** the four flat top views (P5R, P5R fallback, P5O, P5O without the 4th oscillator) and the two standing views. I judged with my hands first (what I reach for, what I set once, what I tweak mid-phrase, what I'd hunt for) and with my eyes second.

The bar is the owner's 10: "I'd play this happily on stage and in the studio, and I'd defend every control's place." None of these is there yet. The bones are good, but a handful of controls are still in places nobody could defend.

A note on "p50": the owner's notes fit both P5R and P5O. Both have the floating MOD and TRACK switches and the same base, and both have the stray OUT PAN. The wide oscillator spread he describes shows up sideways in P5R and top to bottom in P5O without the 4th oscillator. I've treated his notes as applying to all four.

## Scores

| Layout | Lid | Base | Overall | Why, in one line |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P5O** (4 analog oscillators) | 7 | 6 | **7** | The oscillator bank is right. Everything to the right of it is crowded, and the switches float. |
| **P5O without the 4th oscillator** | 6 | 6 | **6** | Same, but three rows are stretched to fill four rows of space, so the oscillators are too far apart. |
| **P5R** | 6 | 6 | **6** | The envelopes read cleanly, but the oscillators are strung across the whole width and the switches are scattered under them. |
| **P5R fallback** | 6 | 6 | **6** | The echo group is tidier. Velocity and touch now sit under TUNING with no header, which is worse. |

The base is the same in all four, so I scored it once.

**Which way to go:** build on **P5O**, and make it the layout the owner described at 20:43, which none of the four actually shows. He asked for the amp envelope "going left to right in the top row on the top case". P5O has the amp envelope as a vertical column, and P5R has it as a row in the middle. That's the first fix.

---

## What works (keep it)

1. **P5O's oscillator bank.** Oscillators stacked on the left, with the octave, wave and pitch knobs in columns, is the Moog bank every player knows. You can see all the pitch knobs in one column and detune by eye. P5R scatters the same knobs across 300 mm, with the tuning knobs at the far right behind a divider. Stack them.
2. **The lid holds the settings and the base holds the play controls.** Cutoff, resonance, envelope amount, the mixer, glide, mod, bend and volume are all on the base, a hand's width from the keys. That's the right instinct, and it's what the owner asked for (volume and mixer on the base). Make it the rule for the whole instrument, and then follow it everywhere (see the out group below).
3. **The mixer sits above the filter on the base.** You read it as signal flow: oscillator levels feed the filter. On a Model D-style ladder you push mixer levels into the filter for grit while you play, so having them right by CUTOFF matters.
4. **Bend at the far left with the mod knob next to it.** That's where the left hand lives. The big round plate is easy to find without looking.
5. **The red CUTOFF is the hero, in the middle of the base.** Correct. I'd find it in the dark.
6. **The sharps sit in the back row and the naturals at the front edge,** in the 2–3 groups. That reads as a keyboard straight away.
7. **The echo and reverb words are good.** DARK in place of BBD is a better word, and so is REPEATS in place of feedback.

## What doesn't work, most important first

### 1. Two switches say "mod off · on" and do different things (all four layouts)
One is on the lid (it sends the mod knob to oscillator pitch). The other is next to CUTOFF on the base (it sends the mod knob to the filter). A first-time player will flip the wrong one, and a player on stage will too. Both switches are really **destinations for the MOD knob**, so they belong next to the MOD knob.

### 2. Neither layout shows the owner's own direction
His words: oscillators on the left, the filter envelope in the middle by the speaker, and the amp group running left to right along the lid's top row. He confirmed on 01:27 that "the amp group" means the amp envelope. P5O is close, but its amp envelope is a column. As a player I can live with the amp envelope in the top row. It's the farthest reach on the lid, but it's set per patch more often than it's ridden. FILTER DECAY is the envelope knob I tweak mid-phrase, and in his layout it sits lower and closer.

### 3. The out group is split across the hinge, and the "pan" label appears twice
VOLUME is on the base, labelled "out". The dry PAN and LINK are on the lid, also labelled "out". The echo PAN on the lid is just "pan". So there are two groups called OUT on two different halves, two knobs called PAN, and the pan nobody touches mid-set is on the lid while its own volume is on the base. That's the owner's "the out pan makes no sense", and he's right. Put **VOLUME · PAN · LINK together on the base**. Once the echo's PAN sits inside the ECHO group, its label is clear on its own.

### 4. Switches are placed by whatever space is left, not by what they do
- **VOICES** (1·3·4) is under oscillator 1 in P5R, and floats at the top centre in P5O. Voice count decides how the keys deal notes to the oscillators. It belongs with VELOCITY and TOUCH in the KEYS group, where you set up how the keyboard plays.
- **TRACK** (oscillator 3 key tracking) floats at the top centre in P5O, three rows away from oscillator 3. It must sit **in oscillator 3's row**. It's the switch you flip to turn oscillator 3 into an LFO, so it belongs at that oscillator's hand.
- **MOD** on the lid: see item 1.
- **P5O labels VOICES "1 · 2 · 4"**, while the other three layouts and the architecture doc say "1 · 3 · 4". Either it's a typo or it's an unexplained change. Settle it.

### 5. The oscillator spacing is uneven, and the 3-row version is stretched
In P5O the row gaps are about 25, 30 and 25 mm (rows 2 and 3 are farther apart than the others). Without the 4th oscillator, the three rows are pushed out to about 35 mm to fill the space. That's the "really far apart" the owner saw. Set one row pitch (about 24–25 mm centre to centre with labels) and keep it whether there are three rows or four. Give the space the 4th row frees to something else; don't spread the bank to fill it.

### 6. The oscillator numbers read like knob markings
The "1, 2, 3, 4" are printed at nine o'clock right against each OCTAVE knob's tick ring. They look like a tick label: octave position 1? Move the numbers into a clear margin column to the left of the bank, aligned with each other, well outside the ticks.

### 7. Knob markings follow no single rule
Here is what I counted on the P5O lid alone:
- **OCTAVE:** 6 long ticks plus a stray short tick, and a dot.
- **WAVE:** 7 short ticks.
- **PITCH:** 7 ticks and a dot, in a different spot.
- **Echo TIME:** an arrow, a triangle and clusters of dots, all unexplained.
- **Echo TYPE:** 4 names, but 5–6 ticks.
- **Reverb TYPE:** a name fan pushed off to the upper right, off centre on the knob.
- **Base knobs:** 7 ticks. **CUTOFF:** 11.

The **MIDI dot** wanders too: at one or two o'clock on OCTAVE, PITCH and TUNE; at the lower right on MOD and GLIDE; after the word on "pan●"; beside the CUTOFF ring; and next to the LINK switch. The architecture says MIDI never flips switches, so a dot on LINK either contradicts that or is an LED that looks like a MIDI dot. A player learns a panel's markings in the first ten minutes. If the rules change from knob to knob, those ten minutes go to waste.

### 8. The type selectors' labels are hard to read under your hand
TAPE / DARK / CLEAN / PINGPONG and PLATE / HALL / SPRING / SHIMMER are staggered on two heights, so you can't tell at a glance which name goes with which detent. The two groups also run in opposite orders: ECHO is type then time, while REVERB is decay then type in P5O. And the reverb types aren't in size order.

### 9. Echo TIME doesn't say what it does in SYNC
In FREE the knob is milliseconds to seconds; in SYNC it picks a note division. The glyphs around the knob are a private code. A player in SYNC needs to know where 1/8 and dotted 1/8 are without counting clicks.

### 10. The base has dead zones, and its rows don't line up
- Row 1 has large gaps between CONTROLLERS, MIXER and EFFECTS.
- Row 2 has empty space about 20 mm wide between GLIDE and RESONANCE, and again between ENVELOPE and VOLUME.
- The columns of the two rows don't line up: the mixer knobs, the filter knobs, and ECHO/REVERB over VOLUME are all staggered.

That's the "less senseful" the owner felt. The base isn't badly organised; it just looks unplanned, so it reads as unplanned.

### 11. The base's left column pairs the wrong things
RELEASE (the Model D decay switch) sits above BEND, and BLEND sits above MOD. BLEND (the mod source mix) with MOD (the depth) is a true pair, so good. But RELEASE has nothing to do with bend. RELEASE and GLIDE are both "how notes join and end", and they're the two you flip or turn together on a lead line.

### 12. The filter appears twice under different headers
In P5O the lid's filter envelope has the header FILTER, and the base's cutoff group also has the header FILTER. In P5R the lid says ENVELOPES while the base's amount knob says ENVELOPE. Each of these is defensible alone; together they're confusing. P5R's ENVELOPES header also sits on top of the grille, so it belongs to neither envelope. The owner flagged that one.

### 13. The TAP button looks like a 26th key
It's a big brass dome, the same finish and nearly the same shape as the natural pegs. In the standing view, the eye groups it with the keyboard. It also lives far from FREE/SYNC and TIME on the lid. TAP on the base is right, because it's a play gesture, but it needs a different face.

### 14. Small things
- **The "big energy" wordmark** still appears on all four. The owner said it doesn't need to say that.
- **TRACK on the base** is labelled off · 1/3 · 2/3. A player who self-oscillates the filter to play it as a sine wants FULL tracking. The architecture already proposes off · ½ · full; take it.
- **EXTRA, with SOURCE stacked above it**, puts SOURCE at a different height from every other label on that row.
- **The brass base knobs** sit right behind brass and black pegs. In the standing view the knob caps and the key caps are almost the same object. That's a look decision for the owner, but it costs some legibility.

## The strongest objections the owner is likely to have
- "You still haven't done my layout." (Item 2.) Show it.
- "The out pan still makes no sense." It won't until PAN moves next to VOLUME.
- "The markings are still inconsistent." Fixing a few knobs won't satisfy him; he'll want one written marking rule applied to every knob.
- "The bottom case looks random." Until the base is on a grid, he'll keep saying this, even though most of its grouping is right.

---

## Suggestions (concrete, in order of payoff)

1. **Build the owner's 20:43 layout on P5O's bones.** On the lid:
   - **Top row:** AMP attack · decay · sustain, left to right, centred over the speaker.
   - **Left of the speaker:** the FILTER envelope column, as in P5O.
   - **Right of the speaker:** ECHO and REVERB. Moving the amp envelope out of the right-hand column gives them a full column of room, which fixes their crowding.
   - **Under the speaker:** the KEYS group (see 4).
2. **Put the mod knob's destinations by the mod knob.** Both destination switches move into the base's CONTROLLERS group, beside MOD, labelled **PITCH** and **FILTER** (on/off each). That removes the duplicate "mod" label and gives the base a clear group: bend, mod, and where mod goes.
3. **Make OUT one group on the base: VOLUME · PAN · LINK.** The lid keeps only the echo's PAN, inside ECHO, where it needs no extra word.
4. **Put each switch with what it changes:**
   - **KEYS** = VOICES · VELOCITY · TOUCH, together under the speaker.
   - **TRACK** goes in oscillator 3's row, as a fourth column on that row.
   - Fix the "1 · 2 · 4" / "1 · 3 · 4" mismatch.
5. **Use one oscillator grid at one pitch, and use the empty slot.** Oscillator 1 has no PITCH knob (true to the Model D), which leaves a hole in row 1. Put **TUNE** in that slot and **DRIFT** next to it as a fourth knob on row 1. That puts master tuning right at the oscillators, as the owner asked, and kills P5O's floating TUNING island and its long rule line. Keep the row pitch the same with three or four oscillators.
6. **Write one marking rule and apply it to every knob:**
   - **Continuous knobs:** 11 ticks, 7 o'clock to 5 o'clock, same radius, same weight.
   - **Stepped knobs** (OCTAVE, both TYPE knobs): one tick per detent, with a word or number at each tick on a single arc at one radius, centred on the knob.
   - **MIDI dot:** always in the same place relative to the label (for example just right of the label's baseline), on knobs only, never on switches.
   - **Oscillator numbers:** in a margin column, outside the ticks.
7. **Fix the type selectors:**
   - Put their names on one arc, centred.
   - Use the same order in both groups: **TYPE first, then TIME or DECAY.**
   - Run the reverbs small to large: SPRING · PLATE · HALL · SHIMMER.
8. **Mark echo TIME for both modes:** small division marks on the inner ring (1/16, 1/8, 1/8 dotted, 1/4, 1/2, 1/1), with FREE's range shown at the ends of the outer ring. Leave out the arrows and triangles.
9. **Put the base on a grid:**
   - Line row 1's columns up over row 2's.
   - Close the two 20 mm dead zones.
   - Swap the left column so **BLEND sits over MOD** and **RELEASE sits over GLIDE**, with BEND alone.
10. **Give TAP its own face.** Use cream, black or a ring, anything except a key-brass dome, and keep it near the effect levels as it is now.
11. **Use one header per idea.** On the lid, use a single ENVELOPES header with FILTER and AMP as row or column labels in the same margin style as the oscillator numbers. Keep it off the grille. The base keeps FILTER for cutoff, resonance and envelope amount.
12. **Housekeeping:**
    - Remove the "big energy" wordmark.
    - Make filter TRACK off · ½ · full.
    - Align SOURCE's label with the rest of the row.

If the team does 1 through 6, I'd expect to be arguing about details, not about where things live. If 7 through 11 are also done cleanly, I expect I'd be arguing for it rather than against it.
