## What changed since round 1, and why
- **No raised dividers, no raised front rail** (owner: "the dividers … got in the way of the knobs"; "that includes the separator between keys and controls too"). Keys, front rail and floors are one flush deck at 24.
- **The walls are still real, now sunk.** The egg-crate walls run under the floors, and their tops stop 1 mm below the floor face. The result is a pale birch rule 3 mm wide in a 1 mm groove: you feel it, you don't hit it (owner: "use another method of separation if you think it's important to feel the sections"). This is the printer's other meaning of a rule, a strip set *in* the forme, not standing above it.
- **The cards** now lie flat in pockets in the front rail, right in front of their sections. Letterpress black on white: a bold serif numeral and a bold sans name, readable in the 3/4 view (director fix 3).
- **Rim:** the cheeks and rear rail are at **36.7 mm, level with CUTOFF** (24.5 + 12.15), with a 1.5 mm outer chamfer. The front is open. Director fix 2 asked for ≤ 38 and level with the knob tops; it's now 1.3 mm under that.
  - I'd sketched a deeper IV floor to drop CUTOFF under a 35 mm rim. I dropped it: it would make a 1.3 mm step down beside IV, and the owner's rule is flat.
- **Knobs: red** (letterpress black and red), with the cream CUTOFF the one odd sort. Director (breadth): different colours per concept.
- **R2 spacing:** a 7 mm gap from any knob to a rule (fix 1). Worst-knob check on sheet B (fix 4): the rule sits 1 mm *below* the floor, so the thumb pad has nothing to hit.
- **Cost ≤ $15 with India ink as the default dye** (fix 5).

## The idea
A printer's type case: a shallow wooden case whose compartments are its circuit sections, with the controls kept in them like sorts.

## Principles
1. **The walls are real, and sunk.** *Because* a type case's dividers hold things: here they're egg-crate walls under the floors, cut 1 mm below the face. A fingertip feels each section, and nothing stands in the knobs' way.
2. **The case keeps its sorts safe.** *Because* that's what a case is for: the cheeks and rear rail rise to the tallest sort (CUTOFF). The front is open, so the hands come straight in.
3. **Inked floors, bare-wood marks.** *Because* engraving through a dyed floor exposes pale wood: every legend comes out light, with no second colour or process (H6).
4. **Labels are case cards.** *Because* a case is labelled with a card: one per section, flat in a pocket in the front rail, letterpressed or hand-lettered, swappable.
5. **Printer's two colours.** *Because* letterpress is black and red: ink floors, red knobs. The one cream sort is CUTOFF, the hero.

## Look
- **Palette:** natural birch (cheeks, rail, rules), India-ink floors, red KN1250 knobs with a cream paint-pen pointer line, a cream 1083 CUTOFF with a red line, white cards with black letterpress, white and black acrylic short keys, an amber status light.
- **Type:**
  - Engraved legends: Inter SemiBold 1.9 mm, pale on black.
  - Cards: numeral 7 mm bold serif (DejaVu Serif Bold in the mock; any bold roman he sets) and name 3.4 mm bold sans. Card I's name is set at 2.1 mm, because its card is 31 mm wide.
- **Silhouette:** a low open tray; at the sides and back the case rises to 36.7, at the front it is level with the keys.

## Feel and feedback
- The sunk rules are the blind-find (J16): a fingertip drops into a groove at every section break.
- **Lights:**
  - status: amber, in V;
  - "not in control": cream, at (205.5, 93) between CUTOFF and EMPHASIS;
  - behaviour as in `jobs.md`: tuning = slow pulse, ready = steady dim warm, fail = blink count, low battery = slow flash in the second colour.
- Keys: Choc V2 silent, 2.8 mm travel, flush with the floors; the sharps stand 2 mm higher, so they can be found.
